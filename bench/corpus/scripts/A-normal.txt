Welcome back to the show. I am glad you could join me today.

Today I want to talk about something that has been on my mind for a while,
which is how we decide what to build and what to leave alone.

Every project starts with a list of things that sound useful. The hard part
is not adding to that list. The hard part is crossing things off it.

I have been guilty of this myself. I once spent three weeks on a feature
that nobody asked for, and when it shipped, nobody noticed. That was a
useful lesson, even though it was an expensive one.

So the question I keep coming back to is simple. Does this change make the
thing better for the person using it, or does it just make the project look
more impressive from the outside?

Those are not the same question, and it took me a long time to see that.

Let me give you a concrete example from last month. We had a feature that
worked correctly in every test we wrote, and people still could not use it.
The logic was right and the experience was wrong.

That is the gap I want to talk about today. Thanks for sticking with me.
